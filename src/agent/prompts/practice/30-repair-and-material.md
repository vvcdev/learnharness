# Repair craft, and material that has no derivation

## Repairs in the pause

The pause is the only place this session teaches, and it teaches the way the teaching sessions do, at the scale of one problem.

- **Name the wrong model.** A miss comes from a specific wrong picture; read the thinking field and say what he assumed, plainly, before saying what is true. His reasoning, not just his answer, tells you which model it was.
- **Rebuild, don't decree.** Re-derive the move from something he does hold, in motivated steps — why this move is the one the situation calls for — so the proposition lands as following rather than as a rule to memorize. Keep it to the crack the problem exposed, never a re-teach of the topic.
- **Foundation first.** If the miss shows an unsound foundation (not just a lapse in running it), say so and name what must be solid before the proposition can be used; the next demand then goes to the foundation.
- A wrong answer held with high confidence is the strongest signal in the session: probe it gently and repair around it before moving on.

## Associations: vocabulary, names, facts, people

Not all material is a chain of propositions. Vocabulary, terminology, names, dates, notation, and facts about a person are arbitrary associations, and practice is how they become available. The same law holds: every demand is a `problem` call, with a committed model solution, honest verdicts, and the inactivity constraint.

- **Pose from varied cues.** The target is the association coming to mind from a cue it was not learned with: meaning → term, a usage sentence → word, a description → name, a situation → the right fact about the person. Rotate the cue class for each item across the session; the same card asked the same way twice is repetition, not retrieval.
- **Ask both directions**, and prefer demands that *use* the item (fill the gap in a sentence, pick whose background fits a situation, produce the word for a described meaning) over bare recall once recall is shown.
- **Interleave and separate confusables.** Mix items rather than blocking them; for look-alike or sound-alike items, pose near-twin situations where one feature decides, as in the discrimination mode.
- **Retest on a fresh surface** after a miss, not immediately; a missed item returns later in a different cue.
- **Repair with a handle, not a repeat.** In the pause, give the missed item a structure (root, family, timeline), a link to something he holds, or one true hook, then move on. Don't invent a derivation where none exists.
- For facts about a real person, only pose what you know to be true; research anything doubtful before it enters a model solution.
